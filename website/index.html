Report it. Route it. Resolve it.

An Android app prototype that takes a citizen's report of a broken road, blocked drain or dead streetlight and follows it to a fix the citizen can verify.

What it does

One shared incident database, with a view for every kind of city fault.

Understands the complaint

A small text model sorts the complaint into a category and shows how sure it is. Staff can always override it, and every override is logged.

Spots duplicates

Reports of the same problem are linked under one parent, so the city sees how many citizens are affected.

Explains the priority

Severity, safety risk, people affected, location, repeat reports and history each add points you can read.

Routes to the right team

Reports go to the department, then an officer who accepts or rejects with a reason. Overdue cases escalate.

Maps and hotspots

Pins, groups and a density heat view show where problems cluster and where they keep coming back.

Closes the loop

Before and after photos, then the citizen says whether it is really fixed. If not, the case reopens.

From complaint to verified fix

Every report follows the same ten steps, with a timestamped history.

  1. Report submitted
  2. AI analysis completed
  3. Duplicate verification
  4. Priority assigned
  5. Department selected
  6. Officer assigned
  7. Work in progress
  8. Resolution evidence uploaded
  9. Citizen verification or feedback
  10. Incident closed

Three roles

Citizen

Report with a photo and GPS, track progress, see problems nearby and add "I see this too", give feedback.

Municipal officer

Work the assigned queue, watch deadlines, add internal notes, request resources, escalate.

Administrator

City overview, departments and officers, heat map, recurring faults, performance and CSV downloads.

Honest status

This is a hackathon prototype, not an official product of the event or its host, and not an emergency service.
  • The text classifier is a small model trained on example complaints written for this project. Its percentage is its own estimate, not a measured accuracy.
  • Priority is an explainable scoring rule, not machine learning. Daily summaries and maintenance insights are statistics and templates, not AI predictions.
  • Photo analysis by a vision model is not included yet; a person confirms what a photo shows.
  • Data currently stays on each device. There is no shared city backend yet.
  • No live city sensors or feeds are connected.
